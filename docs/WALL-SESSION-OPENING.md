# Wall session — opening measurements

**Fired in parallel with the floor's last path lever, per ruling.** Walls proceed against the
landed floor; the floor's passed systems and its anchor are the input, and this document records
what that input actually is now rather than what it was when §6.5 was written.

Nothing is generated yet. This is the derivation §6.5 demands before a wall can be authored, and
it returns two findings, both of which change what a wall family may be built from.

## 1. THE ANCHOR HAS MOVED, and the re-derivation rule fires

§6.5's stack is **floor-relative** — every figure in it is a multiple of the floor's value. Floor
session two recorded that reference as **114.5**. Measured on the family as it now stands, unlit:

| | |
|---|---:|
| whole floor, median | **100.86** |
| stone faces only, median | 113.85 |
| the anchor §6.5 was written against | 114.50 |

**The stone faces have barely moved. The floor has.** What changed is everything between the
stones: the differential-wear pass made off-route joints deep and continuous and dark, and joints
are a large share of the floor's area. A plane-to-plane comparison is about the floor **as seen**,
and as seen it is now a tenth darker than the number §6.5's ratios were written against.

> **This is the re-derivation rule, second firing.** It fired first when Ruling 56 moved the rig
> and left `WALL-RECIPE.md`'s face ÷ top ≈ 0.35 compensating against a rig that no longer existed.
> It fires again here: **a wall top authored at 1.11 × 114.5 would sit 11% above a value the floor
> no longer has**, and §6.5's separation — the whole point of the clause — would be measured from
> the wrong baseline.

**Recommended anchor for the wall session: 100.86, the whole-floor median.** Stone-face median is
the wrong choice for this specific clause: §6.5 separates *planes*, and a viewer comparing a wall
top to the floor is comparing it to the floor including its joints.

## 2. §6.5's WALL FACE IS BELOW THE FLOOR'S PALETTE, and cannot be authored on it

The floor's ladder, derived from the floor's donor material, spans **75.0 … 154.4** in seven rungs.
Applying §6.5's ruled ratios to the measured anchor:

| plane | ratio | value | nearest rung | |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **wall top** | 1.11 | 111.96 | 114.70 | on the ladder |
| floor | 1.00 | 100.86 | 101.47 | on the ladder |
| **wall face** | 0.60 | 60.52 | 75.02 | ⚠ **below the ladder's floor** |
| **wall face** | 0.50 | 50.43 | 75.02 | ⚠ **below the ladder's floor** |

**The wall face cannot be expressed on the floor's palette at either end of §6.5's range.** Both
0.50 and 0.60 land below the darkest value the ladder contains, and quantising them onto it
collapses the face to 75.02 — which is 0.74 × floor, outside the ruled band and only 0.26 below
the floor where the clause asks for 0.40–0.50 below.

This is not a conflict between two rulings. §6.5's figures explicitly *"carry §5's PLACEHOLDER
status"*, and §5's values are PLACEHOLDER precisely so that this moment can resolve them. The
finding is narrower and more useful than "the numbers disagree":

> **The palette must extend downward before a wall face can exist.** The floor's ladder was
> derived from floor donors, and a floor has no reason to contain the darkest value in the scene.
> The wall face is the largest recess in the room — §6.5 derives it from enclosure — and enclosure
> is exactly what the floor's material never had to express.

**What that implies for the wall family, stated so it is not discovered late:** the wall cannot
inherit the floor's ladder. It needs its own, sharing the floor's tint and its rung spacing so the
two materials sit in one palette, but extending below the floor's darkest rung far enough to reach
0.50 × anchor. At the floor's rung spacing of 13.23, reaching 50.4 from 75.0 is **two further
rungs down** — a nine-rung shared ladder rather than seven.
