YARL — FRAME CRITIC — v1.0

RUN AS: fresh `claude -p`, cwd OUTSIDE the repo. No repo access. No project memory.
INPUT: the numbered PNG files in this directory, nothing else. Read every one of them.
This process is NOT given the art bible. It gets the fiction, the tone, and these questions.

You are being shown finished frames from games. They are NOT labelled, they are in no meaningful
order, and you are NOT told which came from where. Do not guess, and do not let a guess change an
answer. Some are at different tile sizes than others; that is not a defect in any of them.

The on-screen buttons, bars and menus are interface. They are out of scope everywhere.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PLACE

The Paths of the Dead: an underworld that is ADMINISTERED, not haunted — a filing system for
souls. In continuous heavy use since before anyone's records begin, and no one has ever cared
for it. Nothing in it is arranged for anyone's benefit.

The upper floors are a contested frontier where a company of orc-soldiers has held a line for
four hundred years and cannot advance it. They repair things endlessly, with rope, driven pins,
hide and salvaged timber, and they do not care how the repairs look. They are extremely good at
it and have never once cared how it looks.

The tone is dry, cold and completely straight. Not spooky-fun. Not adventurous. No sense of
humour about itself.

Each frame is a top-down dungeon seen straight from above, drawn on a square grid, at the size
the game is actually played. A figure stands carrying the only light there is, so the picture
gets darker further from that figure. That is intended and is not a defect.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BAR

You are judging CRAFT, not style. The question behind every answer below is:

    Does this look like a game somebody paid for, or like the free one next to it?

"Doesn't match some other game's look" is not a defect. "Looks unfinished, looks generated,
looks like a placeholder, looks like a filter was run over it" is.

A frame that needs a qualifier — fine, acceptable, promising, close, better than the others —
is a frame you would NOT ship. Say so. If you find no defect anywhere, suspect your own rigour
before crediting the work.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTIONS

Answer every one. Use the exact labels, at the start of a line, followed by a colon. Refer to
images by their file number. Be specific about WHERE you are looking, and quote what you
actually see rather than what you expect to see.

RANK: the file numbers, best-made first, worst-made last, separated by ">". Every image appears
    exactly once. No ties.

BEST: the single file number you ranked first.
BEST_WHY: what specifically is well made in it. Name the thing and where it is. Not "good
    atmosphere" — "the joints vary in depth along a single run, so the stones read as cut rather
    than stamped."

WORST: the single file number you ranked last.
WORST_WHY: what specifically is wrong with it. Name the thing and where it is.

FLAGGED: the file numbers with an OBVIOUS defect — something a person would notice at a glance
    and would be embarrassed to ship. Comma-separated, or NONE. This is not the same as ranking
    last: an image can be last and still have nothing obviously broken in it, and an image can
    be ranked highly and still carry one glaring fault.

SHIP: the file numbers you would ship as they stand, comma-separated, or NONE. Shipping means
    you would put it in front of a paying player and defend it. Hedging is not shipping.

Then, for EVERY image you did not put in SHIP, one block each:

FLIP <n>: the specific, concrete, executable changes that would flip that image to shippable.
    One per line, each beginning with "- ". "Needs more atmosphere" is not a flip list item.
    "The wall's top surface sits at the same value as the lit floor; separate them" is.
