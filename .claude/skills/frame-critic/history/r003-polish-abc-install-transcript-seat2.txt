**FLIP 1:**

- The sprite at (400,485) reads 158 R against a floor of 248 R. The lamp-bearer is darker than the ground it lights. Raise the sprite above its immediate floor value, or the light has no visible origin.
- The pool is the brightest and flattest region in the deck — mean 163 with local-detail std 22.4, versus 25.7 in image 3 on the identical plate. The light is being applied as a brightness lift over the painting. Multiply the plate instead, so the slab joints left of the figure at x 230–330 survive at full pool brightness.
- The pool's edge is a lumpy hard-cut blob: it bulges left to x≈180, is cut short at x≈460, and has a notch bitten out around (350,560). Replace with a falloff that is monotonic with distance from the sprite.
- The crate at (295,640)–(380,720) has a 4px soft gradient on its right edge and no contact shadow — the floor value 10px around it equals the floor 40px away. Give it a 1px hard silhouette and a directional occlusion shadow away from the sprite.
- The long crack from (300,600) to (750,560) is a constant-width bezier that crosses eleven slab joints without jogging, narrowing, or terminating. Break it at joints and vary its width.
- The same crack passes over the top-left corner of the crate and continues out the other side. A ground crack cannot cross a raised object. Clip it at the silhouette.
- The wall run's top and bottom edges are ruler-straight and unbroken for 320px. Chip the course line — this is a wall repaired for four hundred years.
