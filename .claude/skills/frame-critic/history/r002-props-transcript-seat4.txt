RANK: 1 > 3 > 4 > 2

BEST: 1

BEST_WHY: The masonry in the lit room actually reads as laid stone rather than a stamped texture. Around (460,500)–(620,580) the courses are different heights, the vertical joints are staggered between courses, and individual blocks carry different interior values — some blocks are double-width and one at (560,540) is half-height, so the run reads as cut and fitted. The crack network is the second thing done right: the line entering at (300,760) runs continuously up to (500,470), changes direction where it crosses joints, and does not restart at tile boundaries — it was drawn across the floor, not per-tile. Third, the corridor at x≈500–575: the light gradient runs the full length of the shaft and dies at the sill, and the taut lines crossing it at y≈240 and y≈290 each sag at a different angle with a thicker dark blob where they meet the wall — that reads as rope tied off on driven pins, not as a decal strip.

WORST: 2

WORST_WHY: It has no light model at all. The stone at the bottom-left corner (30,270) sits at the same value as the dirt beside the figure (170,20) — a scene that is supposed to darken away from a carried lamp is uniformly lit corner to corner. Under that, the dirt field is a single 16px tile stamped without variation: the identical hooked dark-red crack sits at the top-left of every tile in the field — it repeats at (200,30), (216,30), (232,30) across the entire brown area. The water is a flat rectangle whose edge runs dead straight down x≈248 and turns a hard 90° at (248,190), with one unvarying cell texture from bank to centre and no wet edge. The lily pads at (325,20) and (313,113) are pure saturated green in a scene whose next-brightest chroma is a muted tan. Nothing casts a shadow — the barrels at (130,203) and (177,203) meet the ground on a hard sprite edge and float. The figure at (225,8) is clipped by the top frame edge, head cut off.
