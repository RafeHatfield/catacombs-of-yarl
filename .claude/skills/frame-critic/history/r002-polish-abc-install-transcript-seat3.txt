**RANK:** 2 > 4 > 3 > 1

**BEST:** 2

**BEST_WHY:** The crack network on the floor is authored, not scattered. At (314,602) there is a single impact node with four cracks radiating from it, and each one is drawn as a hard 1px stair-step on the pixel grid — no anti-aliasing, no smooth bezier laid over pixel art. Where a crack crosses a slab joint at roughly (350,575) it kicks sideways by a pixel instead of running straight through, which is what a fracture does when it meets a seam. The slab joints themselves vary: some are a single dark pixel, some are a 2px doubled line, and a few slabs around (280,560) abut with almost no joint at all, so the floor reads as laid stone rather than a stamped grid. The repair language is present and specific — a driven pin at (365,585) rendered as a vertical bar with a wedge head, and groups of incised tally scores at (600,545) and hatch scores at (640,480) cut at slightly different angles per group. And the light falloff at the pool's right edge, (430,440)–(560,640), is a genuinely smooth gradient with no banding and no stepping.

**WORST:** 1

**WORST_WHY:** The two barrels at (128,200) and (176,200) are pixel-identical copies of one sprite placed side by side, and both are **sliced flat by the dirt/flagstone seam at y=214** — the bottom third of each barrel body is simply gone, cut by a terrain edge on a dead-straight horizontal line. That is a masking bug, not a style choice. Beneath it, the flagstone row at y=214–265 repeats one tile thirteen times across the full frame width with zero variation: the same pale squiggle scratch sits at the same sub-tile offset in every single stone. The brick course at y=265–288 does the same, and one brick at roughly (163,271)–(188,281) is rendered a step lighter than every other brick in the run for no reason — a single wrong tile in the atlas. The water at (248,0)–(344,191) is a perfect rectangle: hard vertical sides, a hard horizontal bottom, a clean 90° corner at (248,191), no bank, no shoreline, no wet margin on the dirt, no depth shading — it abuts the flagstone at x=344 on a straight seam with no lip. The dirt field's left edge at x=56 is likewise a knife-clean vertical cut against flagstone with no transition tile. The two crates at (63,2)–(97,43) and (63,49)–(97,90) are the same sprite twice, same lid, same diamond keyhole in the same position, and neither has a contact shadow. The black brick band at (10,123)–(56,143) is a three-tile wall fragment floating in the middle of the floor with no ends, no returns and no connection to anything, with an orange-and-black striped bar at (26,125)–(35,147) sitting on it and a brown pole running out of its underside down to y≈170 — an object that reads as nothing.

**FLAGGED:** 1, 2, 3, 4

- **2, 3, 4** (shared) — the figure is a **side-profile sprite standing in a plan-view room.** At (378,455)–(440,515) you see a face in profile with a nose and one eye, a sword held out horizontally at shoulder height, a round shield presented face-on, and two boots viewed from the side. Every other object in the frame — floor slabs, wall runs, the crate at (310,645) — is drawn straight down. Two projections in one image. Compounding it: **he is carrying no light.** Both hands are full, sword and shield, no torch, no lantern. The entire lighting premise of the frame is a figure carrying the only light there is. He also receives nothing from the lamp — flat cel shading, saturated cyan and orange against a frame where nothing else exceeds about 20% saturation, a hard black 1px outline where nothing else is outlined, and no contact shadow under the boots. - Author at least four variants of the flagstone tile used in the row at y=214–265 and randomise placement; the pale scratch currently sits at an identical sub-tile offset in all thirteen stones.
- Correct the single light brick at (163,271)–(188,281) to the same value as the rest of the course at y=265–288.
- Build an edge set for the dirt/flagstone boundary: the vertical cut at x=56 and the horizontal cut at y=214 need transition tiles with dirt spilling a few pixels onto the stone and stone showing through the dirt, plus scatter at the corner (56,214).
- Build a shoreline for the water at (248,0)–(344,191): a darker wet margin two to three pixels wide on the dirt side, a value ramp into the centre so the pool has depth, and break the dead-straight bottom edge at y=191 so it does not terminate on a 90° corner.
- Vary or remove the duplicated lily-pad sprite — the same pad with the same white asymmetric stem mark appears unrotated at (355,20), (330,110), (348,122) and (275,163).
- Break up the dirt fill between x=56–344: the red speckle cluster repeats on a visible regular lattice down the whole field.
- Resolve the wall fragment at (10,123)–(56,143) — give it ends and a connection to another wall, or remove it; and replace the orange striped bar at (26,125)–(35,147) with something that reads as an object.
- Add distance-from-figure falloff. The frame is uniformly lit edge to edge with no contact shadows anywhere, which is why every object reads as a decal on a flat plane.
- Bring the orc at (207,0)–(233,25) into the environment's key. Its green is the most saturated colour in the frame by a wide margin and sits in no other element.
- Redraw the figure in the same projection as the room, or accept the mismatch deliberately — currently it is a front-facing sprite (both eyes, both arms, both legs visible) on a plan-view floor.

Along (430,440)–(560,640) the boundary steps in whole lit/unlit squares; the falloff needs to be evaluated per pixel, or the tile mask needs blurring before it multiplies the frame. The diff against 2 shows the added light has hard block edges throughout its extent.

- All FLIP 2 items on the figure and the black rooms apply here unchanged.

---

Worth saying plainly: 2, 3 and 4 are one frame with three light passes. Judging them as three frames flatters the set — the underlying environment art is made once, and it is the strongest thing in the deck. The recurring failure across all three is that the lighting model treats walls and floors as the same surface, and the figure was authored for a different game than the room he is standing in. Fix those two and 2 ships; 3 and 4 are then light-pass parameters, not art problems.
