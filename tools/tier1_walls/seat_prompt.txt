YARL — BLIND SEAT — THE WALL AND THE DARK BEYOND IT — v0.2

RUN AS: fresh `claude -p`, cwd OUTSIDE the repo. No repo access. No project memory.
INPUT: the PNG file(s) in this directory, nothing else.
This process is NOT given the art bible. It gets the fiction, the tone, and these questions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A top-down dungeon seen straight from above, drawn on a square grid, shown at the size the game
is actually played on a phone. A figure stands in the middle carrying the only light there is,
so the picture gets darker the further it is from that figure. That is intended.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PLACE

The Paths of the Dead: an underworld that is ADMINISTERED, not haunted — a filing system for
souls. In continuous heavy use since before anyone's records begin, and no one has ever cared
for it. Nothing in it is arranged for anyone's benefit.

The upper floors are a contested frontier where a company of orc-soldiers has held a line for
four hundred years and cannot advance it. They repair things endlessly, with rope, driven pins,
hide and salvaged timber, and they do not care how the repairs look. They are extremely good at
it and have never once cared how it looks.

The tone is dry, cold and completely straight. Not spooky-fun. Not adventurous. No sense of
humour about itself.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SCOPE — READ THIS AND HOLD TO IT

**THE SUBJECT IS THE STANDING STRUCTURE** — the walls, whatever they are made of, and the
darkness beyond them.

**THE GROUND IS FINISHED WORK AND IS OUT OF SCOPE.** It was judged in an earlier round. Do not
comment on the floor's material, its cracks, its wear or its colour, and do not let it influence
anything you say about what stands on it. If the only thing you can find to say is about the
floor, say that instead.

The on-screen buttons and bars are the game's interface and are also out of scope.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTIONS

Answer every one. Use the exact labels. Be specific about WHERE you are looking — give pixel
regions or describe the location — and quote what you actually see rather than what you expect.

Q1: WHAT IS STANDING AROUND THIS ROOM MADE OF? Name the material in a few words, as if telling
    someone what they would put a hand on. If you cannot tell, say so plainly.
Q1_WHY: what in the image tells you that.

Q2: IS THAT SOLID MASS, OR A FLAT PATTERN AROUND THE EDGE? Pick one, then say what made you pick
    it. If you cannot tell whether the room is bounded by something with thickness or by a
    change of colour, say that — it is a real answer.

Q3: WHERE EXACTLY DOES THE GROUND STOP? Point at the transition. Describe what happens across
    it — over how many pixels, and what changes.

Q4: WHAT IS HOLDING THIS PLACE TOGETHER, AND WHAT WOULD FALL DOWN IF YOU CUT IT? Name the
    specific thing you would cut and what it is holding. If nothing in the image is visibly
    holding anything, say NOTHING — that is a real and useful answer.

Q5: DOES THIS LOOK LIKE SOMETHING THAT WAS BUILT, OR LIKE AN IMAGE THAT WAS REPEATED? One or the
    other, then why.

Q6: WOULD YOU BELIEVE THIS SHIPPED IN A COMMERCIAL GAME? Answer YES or NO. Then, separately:
    is it merely competent, or is it genuinely good? "Fine", "acceptable", "solid" and
    "promising" all mean NO to the second question.

Q7: AT THE SIZE SHOWN, WITHOUT ZOOMING IN, WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY SEE ON THE STANDING SURFACES —
    not the layout, not the outline, but the face of the material itself? List what is legible at
    1:1 and what only appears once you magnify. Say whether that changes with distance from the
    figure, and where. If a surface is blank at 1:1, say so plainly; if something is there but
    you had to hunt for it, that counts as hunting, not as seeing.
Q7_WHY: name the pixel regions you checked at 1:1, and separately the ones you had to magnify.

Q8: IS THERE ANYTHING YOUR EYE KEEPS RETURNING TO? Anything you notice more than once, anything
    that starts to feel like a pattern, anything at the same place in every cell. Say exactly
    what and exactly where. If there is nothing, say NOTHING.

Q9: WHAT IS BEYOND THE WALLS? Describe it in your own words. Is it more of the same stuff, is it
    empty space, is it another room you cannot see into, or is it something you cannot name? Say
    which, and what in the image told you.

Q10: COULD YOU TELL A WAY THROUGH FROM A DEAD END WITHOUT WALKING INTO IT? Find the narrow gap
     in the structure north of the figure. Say whether you read it as a passage leading somewhere
     or as a notch in a pattern, and what made the difference. If you cannot find it, say so.

Q11: HAS ANYTHING HAPPENED TO THIS STRUCTURE? Describe its condition in your own words, and say
     what you read it from.

Q12: BETWEEN THE LIT FACE OF THE STRUCTURE AND THE DARKNESS THERE IS A DARKER BAND. WHAT IS IT —
     THE TOP OF A THICK WALL SEEN FROM ABOVE, OR MORE GROUND? Pick one, or say CANNOT TELL, then
     say what decided it. If you think it is ground, say whether it is lit ground or unlit ground.
     Then, whichever you picked: does that band look like one continuous surface, or does it
     break into squares? Say where you see the breaks if there are any.

CULL: If this should be rejected, give the single strongest reason in under twenty words.
      If it should not be rejected, write NONE.
      A critic who finds no defect should suspect its own rigour before crediting the work. Look
      again before writing NONE.

FLIP LIST
Then a list, one per line starting with "- ", of the specific changes you would require before
this could ship. Concrete and visual. No more than five. If there are none, write "- none".
