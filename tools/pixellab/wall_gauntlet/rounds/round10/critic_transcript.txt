FLIP LIST
- r10_05: extend the beam's metal plate past the beam's top and bottom edges into the masonry courses above and below, and terminate each end in two visible driven pin heads, so the plate ties timber to stone instead of decorating the timber.
- r10_05: the cap band's pale flecks repeat as one identical motif at x=5–8 and x=19–21; redraw the flecks with different widths and spacings at each position so no two are the same shape, and break the 13px pitch.
- r10_05: replace the arbitrary rectangular tone patches inside each block with damage that has a cause — spalling that starts at a mortar joint and widens outward, or a chipped corner — so the variation reads as wear rather than as dithering.
- r10_01, r10_04, r10_06: every block is a single flat colour. Give each block two or three internal values keyed to its own edges (darker where it meets a joint, lighter across a worn face), so a block reads as a stone rather than as a filled rectangle.
- r10_01: the olive band at rows 26–28 is a stripe. Add a darker one-pixel underside along its full length to give it thickness, and put a driven pin or a hide strap through it at least twice across the 32px so it is held on rather than drawn on.
- r10_00, r10_04: delete the flat single-colour bands at the bottom of the tile and carry the masonry coursing all the way to row 31, so the tile has no dead area when stacked.
- r10_00, r10_08: the vertical bar fields are one row of pixels extruded downward for ten or more rows. Vary each bar's width by a pixel at two or three heights and break at least one bar with a splice or a lashing, so it reads as timber that was cut and fitted rather than as a stencil.
- r10_08: remove the teal cluster entirely and remove the purple flecks from the cap band; both introduce hues the rest of the tile does not support.
- r10_03, r10_09: remove the pale vertical smears. If a stain running down the wall is wanted, start it at a specific source — under a beam end, out of a joint — and make it darker than the wall, not lighter than everything else in the tile.
- r10_09: break the even spacing of the tan squares — vary their widths, drop one, plug another with a different material — so the band stops reading as moulding and starts reading as a run of salvaged sockets nobody aligned.
- r10_07: strip the black rings from the two pale squares and set them into the surface with a one-pixel recess on two adjacent sides only, so they read as plates driven flush rather than as stickers.
- All candidates: the bright cap band is a hard horizontal edge at exactly the same row in every tile, so stacking produces a bright stripe every 32 pixels. Ragged the boundary between cap and face by one or two pixels at irregular intervals and let a block or a beam end break upward through it.
