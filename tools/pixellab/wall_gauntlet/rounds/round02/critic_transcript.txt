FLIP LIST
- Nothing in this set carries a single repair. Put the orc work in: a salvaged timber baulk laid across the face, and at each end of it a driven iron pin with a visible head and a dark bruise in the stone where it was hammered home. The beam must have thickness — a lit-side face and a darker end-grain edge — not a stripe.
- No candidate answers W3. Give each tile a top surface: a narrow 3–4 pixel band along the top edge, one flat value distinct from the front face, terminated by a single hard pixel line where the top meets the front. That band is what makes it a wall you see the side of.
- Fix the wrap on r02_07 and r02_00. Column 31 must continue into column 0 — move the full-height dark bar off the edge into the tile interior, or split it so half sits at x=0 and half at x=31 and the two halves meet.
- Fix the vertical wrap on r02_09 and r02_02. The pale band currently sits entirely inside the bottom rows; split it so its lower half occupies rows 30–31 and its upper half rows 0–1, so stacking reassembles one continuous course instead of a stripe with a hard step above it.
- Break the period on r02_05 and r02_00. The horizontal units are all the same width and all on the same baseline. Vary block length by 2–5 pixels, drop one course by a pixel, and knock a corner off two or three stones so the eye stops reading a woven pattern.
- Raise contrast on r02_04 and r02_06. Mortar joints currently sit within 15 levels of the stone face; push joints down to roughly a third of face value and add a one-pixel lighter arris on the upper edge of each stone so blocks separate at 32-pixel viewing size.
- Delete the saturated green from r02_03 and the warm browns from r02_plant. Nothing in the Paths is green or fresh; pull those hues into the same cold blue-grey range as the rest of the set.
- Remove the centred features from r02_02 and r02_03 entirely. A wall tile has no centre — redistribute anything you want to keep so that no element is complete within one tile.
- On r02_08, either run the bright vertical post to both the top and bottom edges so it continues into its neighbours as a full-height stud, or cut it and replace it with a lashing of rope crossing two stones.
