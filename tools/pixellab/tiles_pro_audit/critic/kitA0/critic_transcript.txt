FLIP LIST
- Nothing in this set is fixed to anything. Every timber crosses over another timber and stops. Redraw each crossing with a visible fixing at the intersection: a 2×2 pale pin head with a one-pixel dark shadow notch below it, or a two-pixel band of rope wrapped over both members with the wrap direction visible. A structural post must show a fixing at its head and its foot, not just terminate.
- The pale top band is flush with the wall face, so it reads as a lighter patch of texture, not a horizontal surface. Give the cap a near edge: one row of the cap's own colour, then a one-pixel dark line, then two to three rows of a distinct third value for the front lip of the cap stones, before the wall face begins. That single lip is what makes a lantern sit on it.
- Wall thickness is not conserved around corners. In cand_01, cand_04, cand_17, cand_22, cand_27, cand_39 the arm that runs left–right shows a slab of top surface and the arm that runs toward the viewer shows none. Draw the cap turning the corner — carry the cap band down the near arm as a foreshortened strip of the same depth, with the mitre or the overlap of the two cap runs visible at the inside angle.
- The doorway pieces (cand_21, cand_34) show a passage with no wall thickness. Draw the reveal: two to four native pixels of returning face on each jamb, in a value between the cap and the wall face, so the opening reads as cut through something solid. Set the head beam into the jambs rather than in front of them, and put a pin or a strap where it lands.
- At 32 pixels the timber lattice on cand_03, cand_05, cand_06, cand_07, cand_10, cand_12, cand_18, cand_19, cand_26, cand_28, cand_30, cand_32, cand_35, cand_38 collapses into undifferentiated brown-on-grey crosshatch and no role is readable. Cut the timber count to two or three members per tile, make each one at least three pixels thick with a distinct top edge, and leave the stone between them plain so the silhouette of the repairs is legible at ship size.
- The set is not forty pieces. cand_13 and cand_33 are byte-identical; cand_22 and cand_39 are 100% identical across their whole overlap; cand_05/cand_12, cand_29/cand_32, cand_10/cand_18 are 99%+ identical; 105 of 715 overlapping pairs are 90%+ identical. Author each role from scratch instead of re-cropping one master painting, so a corner is drawn as a corner rather than as a rectangle cut out of a length.
- cand_24 contains two disconnected islands of wall with transparent space between them, and cand_18 has a rectangular hole of transparency punched through its top surface. Close both silhouettes, or if the hole is meant to be damage, draw a broken edge for it — a ragged one-pixel dark line, exposed rubble core in a third value, and displaced stones at the lip.
- cand_00 is a different palette from everything else here, repeats exactly every 32 pixels vertically, and shows a wall in perfect condition with mortar as its only fastening. Rebuild it in the grey-and-brown family, break the vertical repeat by moving at least four stones per 32-row period, and drive at least one repair through it — a pinned timber, a rope-bound course, a stone replaced with something salvaged and the wrong size.
- cand_09 is not a wall. It is a free-standing lump on a diamond footprint in a peach-and-red palette that appears nowhere else in the set. Remove it.
- cand_11, cand_16, cand_20, cand_23, cand_37 are fragments under 300 native pixels with no cap, no end and no joint. Either delete them or give each one the single feature that defines its role — a capped end with a visible lip, or a socket where the neighbouring piece keys into it.
