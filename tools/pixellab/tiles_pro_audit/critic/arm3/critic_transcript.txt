FLIP LIST
- Draw a top plane on every piece. Reserve the top 8–10 rows of each tile for the wall's upper surface, drawn as the *ends* of the wall stones — short rectangles running across the wall's thickness, not the long face-on running-bond currently used — and put a single hard 1px lip line where that top meets the front face. Right now the entire directory except cand_00 is flat front elevation, so no piece answers the thickness question.
- Redraw the top surface separately for each run direction. A 12-wide north–south piece (cand_02, 13, 15, 31, 33) must show a long top band running down the screen with the stone ends reading vertically; it currently shows the same horizontal courses as an east–west piece, which is impossible in this view.
- Give every silhouette cut a return face. On cand_04, 17, 22, 27, 28, 36, 39 and on both jambs of cand_18, 21, 24, 34, add a 2–3px vertical strip of top-surface value along the cut edge with a hard dark line at the corner, so the eye sees the wall turn instead of the artwork ending mid-stone.
- Redraw each footprint from scratch instead of masking one painting. cand_10/18 share 98.2% of their pixels, cand_17/27 99.2%, cand_21/34 99.0%, cand_29/32 97.9%, and cand_13/33 are byte-identical; laying five different tiles in a row reproduces the same brace, the same pale bar and the same X mark once every 32 pixels. Move the brace, the plank and the pale bar to different heights and different columns in each piece, and delete cand_33 outright.
- Open up the value range in the brown family. Every brown tile tops out at luminance 131 with 50% of its pixels inside a ~25-level band. Push the stone faces up to around 170 and the joints and recesses down below 25 so that at 32px the coursing still separates; the current spread turns the lower two-thirds of every piece into one brown rectangle.
- Replace the stepped one-pixel diagonals with real timber. The diagonal brace in cand_03/05/06/07/10/18/24/28/30/38 is a single-pixel staircase with no ends; make it 3px thick, give it a lighter top edge and a darker underside, and terminate each end against a stone with a visible driven pin or a lashed rope wrap.
- Put the repair story where it can be seen. In each piece, replace two or three of the dark unreadable clumps with one legible act of repair at a size that survives 32px: a 6×10 salvaged plank pinned flat over a missing stone with four dark pin-heads, or a rope wrap of 3–4 parallel light strokes around a post. Nothing currently in the brown family reads as rope, hide, or a driven pin.
- Cut cand_11, 16, 20, 23 and 37 and redraw them. Each is a flat field with random dark rectangles and no coursing, no edges and no ends; a post needs a visible cap, two visible sides, and a base where it meets the floor.
- Redraw cand_00 so it stops repeating and starts showing use. Its rows 0–28 and 29–57 are pixel-identical: break that by shifting the tan course's cream blocks and the brick bond in the second half. Then remove the ornamental cream-blocked band entirely, replace three or four brick faces with mismatched salvaged stone, chip two arrises, and break up the flat single-colour cap with stone joints and two or three worn hollows.
- Cut cand_09. It is a free-standing rounded object with a dark ring around its lower edge and a saturated orange-cream palette that appears in no other file in the directory.
