FLIP LIST
- The set is one painting with rectangles masked out of it: cand_13 and cand_33 are byte-identical, and cand_03/05/06/07/10/12/18/30/38 differ from each other by 3–12% of their pixels. Draw the pieces separately — at minimum three genuinely different 32-wide wall runs whose brick coursing, crack positions and repair placement do not line up when set side by side, so a three-square corridor does not repeat the same diagonal bar three times at the same height.
- No piece states a top surface. On every piece whose top edge is the top of the wall, draw a distinct cap band 5–7 pixels deep: change the block proportion (wider and shallower than the face bricks, joints running across not down), lift its value 25–35 levels above the face, and put a single hard 1-pixel dark line where the cap meets the face as the front lip. Right now the top band is 10–15 levels lighter and uses the identical 7-pixel course pitch, so it reads as one more course.
- The whole family lives in luminance 37–110 with a standard deviation of 16. Rebuild the value structure: put the timber post at 130–150, the horizontal rails at 105–120, the stone face at 60–80, and the mortar and iron at 30–45. The post currently sits 5–10 levels above the wall behind it and survives only on two 1-pixel dark lines.
- Nothing in this set is fastened. Draw the fixings: two square pin heads where each horizontal rail crosses the post, a driven wedge at the post foot, an iron strap wrapping the post with visible strap ends and pin, and a plate with two pins at each end of the diagonal brace. As drawn the brace ends mid-brick and the rails pass behind the post.
- Every step, notch and end in the silhouette is a straight cut through whole bricks. Where a piece ends or turns, draw the return: a column of quoin stones alternating long and short, a timber jamb post on the cut face, and change the brick coursing on the last 4 pixels so the wall visibly finishes rather than being clipped.
- cand_18, cand_21, cand_24 and cand_34 have transparent rectangles in the interior of the wall face that sever timber rails mid-span. Either remove those holes, or if they are openings, draw them as openings: a head timber, two jamb posts, a sill, and cut ends on the severed rail that land on the jambs.
- cand_09 is a diamond in a cream-and-maroon palette with a rim 72 levels darker than its interior. Redraw it as a square-footprint post cap in the set's 23-colour palette, with the dark rim removed and the top face lifted in value instead.
- cand_00 is a different set: 7 colours, cool blue-grey, pure black mortar, no timber, no iron, no repair, every joint intact. Either drop it or rebuild it in the family palette with the post, rails and at least one visible repair.
- cand_11 (12×9), cand_37 (12×9) and cand_20 (12×16) carry no structure at all. Either give each one a single legible element that names its role — a strap end with two pins, a rail terminating on a plate, a quoin corner — or cut them from the set.
