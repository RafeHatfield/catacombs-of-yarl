YARL — BLIND SEAT — THE FLOOR — v0.1

RUN AS: fresh `claude -p`, cwd OUTSIDE the repo. No repo access. No project memory.
INPUT: the PNG file(s) in this directory, nothing else.
This process is NOT given the art bible. It gets the fiction, the tone, and these questions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A top-down dungeon seen straight from above, drawn on a square grid, shown at the size the game
is actually played on a phone. A figure stands in the middle carrying the only light there is.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PLACE

The Paths of the Dead: an underworld that is ADMINISTERED, not haunted — a filing system for
souls. In continuous heavy use since before anyone's records begin, and no one has ever cared
for it. Nothing in it is arranged for anyone's benefit.

The upper floors are a contested frontier where a company of orc-soldiers has held a line for
four hundred years and cannot advance it. They repair things endlessly, with rope, driven pins,
hide and salvaged timber, and they do not care how the repairs look.

The tone is dry, cold and completely straight. Not spooky-fun. Not adventurous. No sense of
humour about itself.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SCOPE — READ THIS AND HOLD TO IT

**THE WALLS IN THIS IMAGE ARE PLACEHOLDER PROGRAMMER ART.** They are flat coloured blocks
standing in for tiles nobody has drawn yet. They are deliberately garish so that no one can
mistake them for finished work. Do not judge them, do not comment on their colour, and do not
let them influence anything you say about the ground.

**THE SUBJECT IS THE FLOOR** — the walkable ground the figure is standing on and walking
through. That is the only thing being judged.

The on-screen buttons and bars are the game's interface and are also out of scope.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTIONS

Answer every one. Use the exact labels. Be specific about WHERE you are looking — give pixel
regions or describe the location — and quote what you actually see rather than what you expect.

Q1: WHAT IS THIS FLOOR MADE OF? Name the material in a few words, as if telling someone what
    they are standing on. If you cannot tell, say so plainly.
Q1_WHY: what in the image tells you that.

Q2: WHICH WAY WOULD YOU WALK, AND WHY? The figure can go in more than one direction. Pick one,
    and say what about the GROUND made you pick it. If nothing about the ground influenced you
    and you chose at random, say that instead — that is a real and useful answer.

Q3: HAS ANYTHING HAPPENED TO THIS FLOOR? Describe its condition in your own words.
Q3_WHY: what specifically you are reading.

Q4: IS THERE ANYTHING YOUR EYE KEEPS RETURNING TO? Anything you notice more than once, anything
    that starts to feel like a pattern, anything that pulls attention away from the scene. Say
    exactly what and exactly where. If there is nothing, say NOTHING.

Q5: DOES THIS LOOK LIKE A SURFACE THAT WAS MADE, OR LIKE AN IMAGE THAT WAS REPEATED? One or the
    other, then why.

Q6: WOULD YOU BELIEVE THIS SHIPPED IN A COMMERCIAL GAME? Answer YES or NO. Then, separately:
    is it merely competent, or is it genuinely good? "Fine", "acceptable", "solid" and
    "promising" all mean NO to the second question.

Q7: AT THE SIZE SHOWN, WITHOUT ZOOMING IN, WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY SEE ON THE SURFACE ITSELF —
    not the layout, not the joints between pieces, but the face of the material? List what is
    legible at 1:1 and what only appears once you magnify. If the faces are blank at 1:1, say so
    plainly; if something is there but you had to hunt for it, that counts as hunting, not as
    seeing.
Q7_WHY: name the pixel regions you checked at 1:1, and separately the ones you had to magnify.

Q8: HOW OLD IS THIS FLOOR, AND WHAT TELLS YOU? Give an age or an age range in your own terms —
    laid last year, a century, older than the records — and then the specific evidence you read
    it from. If different parts of the floor read as different ages, say which parts and how you
    can tell. If it reads as one age everywhere, say that.
Q8_WHY: name the pixel regions your answer comes from.

Q8B: MARK THE MOST-WALKED GROUND, FROM THE FLOOR ALONE. Before you answer anything about routes,
    give the pixel regions of the ground you judge to be walked MOST, and the ground you judge
    walked LEAST or never. Judge from the FLOOR SURFACE ONLY — you may not use the walls, the
    openings, the shape of the lit area, or where the figure is standing. If the floor gives you
    nothing to go on, write "NO BASIS" rather than guessing from the layout: a wrong mark and an
    honest refusal are both useful, and a guess dressed as a reading is not.
Q8B_WHY: name what in the surface itself put your mark there — value, colour, texture, the joints,
    debris — and say how confident you are.

Q9: YOU ARE STANDING ON THE ROUTE — CAN YOU SEE WHERE IT GOES, TO THE EDGE OF YOUR LIGHT? The
    figure in this image is carrying the only lamp, and it is standing on ground that is walked
    daily. Look at the ground you can actually see, out to where the light gives up. Does it
    continue in a direction? Say which way, and say whether the GROUND told you or whether you
    read it off the walls. If the ground told you nothing, say so plainly — that is a real answer.
    Do NOT judge the ground beyond the light: it is not meant to be legible there.
Q9_WHY: name the pixel regions along your route, and the regions you rejected, and say what is
    different about the ground between them.

Q10: WHICH WAY IN OR OUT OF THIS PLACE IS USED MOST, AND WHICH IS USED LEAST OR NEVER? Judge only
    from the ground in front of each opening. If they all look the same, say so.

CULL: If this floor should be rejected, give the single strongest reason in under twenty words.
      If it should not be rejected, write NONE.
      A critic who finds no defect should suspect its own rigour before crediting the work. Look
      again before writing NONE.

FLIP LIST
Then a list, one per line starting with "- ", of the specific changes you would require before
this floor could ship. Concrete and visual. No more than five. If there are none, write "- none".
