You are looking at three images of floor. You have not seen any of them before and you are not
told which is which, who made them, or in what order they were made.

  A, B and C are in your working directory.

One is a published commercial tile set of long standing. One is an earlier version of a floor in
development. One is the current version of that floor. WHICH IS WHICH IS NOT STATED AND YOU MUST
NOT GUESS FROM CONTEXT — judge only what is in front of you.

Answer these, in this order, with the labels exactly as written.

Q1: RANK THEM BY HOW WELL MADE THEY ARE, best first, as "A > B > C" or with ties as "A = B > C".
Q1_WHY: two or three sentences. Name what decided it — the material, the light, the construction,
    the wear, the repetition, whatever actually moved you.

Q2: WOULD YOU SHIP THE CURRENT ONE? You do not know which it is, so answer for EACH of the three:
    "A: yes/no", "B: yes/no", "C: yes/no". Shipping means: this frame goes into a commercial game
    and you are content to have your name on it.
Q2_WHY: for every "no", one sentence on what stops it.

Q3: DOES ANY OF THEM LOOK LIKE A DECAL — a treatment laid ON the floor rather than the floor
    itself being worn? Name it and say what gives it away, or write NONE.

Q4: DOES ANY OF THEM SHOW ITS GRID? A tile boundary you can see because a treatment stops there,
    a repeat you can predict, a seam. Name it and where, or write NONE.

Q5: PICK ANY TWO AND SAY WHICH IS THE SAME FLOOR AS THE OTHER, IF ANY. Two versions of one
    surface share a material even when one is better made. If you can see that relationship, say
    which pair and what they share; if you cannot, say so plainly.

CULL: If any of these floors should be rejected outright, name it and give the single strongest
      reason in under twenty words. If none should be, write NONE.
      A critic who finds no defect should suspect its own rigour before crediting the work.

⚠ POSITIONS, if you give any: relative to a figure if one is present, in tiles, or as a fraction
  of the image ("the upper third", "the left quarter"). NOT in pixel coordinates — three separate
  seats have given confident pixel coordinates that did not correspond to what they described.
