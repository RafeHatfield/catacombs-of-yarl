Cut sandstone flagstones, mortared, laid in courses like brickwork. Sandy tan under the torch, cold brown out at range.

**Q2: WHICH WAY WOULD YOU WALK, AND WHY?**
South, down the corridor mouth at the bottom of the room (x ≈ 380–450). **Nothing about the ground influenced that.** I picked it off the architecture — it's the one opening whose shape I can actually read.

**Q3: HAS ANYTHING HAPPENED TO THIS FLOOR?**
It's been shot through with holes and scribbled on, evenly, everywhere, by nothing in particular.

There are roughly twenty punched-through black holes across a floor of about 92 tiles — one every four or five tiles — plus a dense mesh of thin cracks in two colours running over everything. It is comprehensively damaged and completely uninformative. Nothing has *happened* to it in the sense of an event: there's no impact centre, no spall radiating from anything, no collapse edge, no subsidence, no patch, no repair. For a floor held by a company that repairs things endlessly with rope, pins, hide and salvaged timber, there is not one pin, plank, or rope on it. Nobody has ever touched this ground.

**Q5: DOES THIS LOOK LIKE A SURFACE THAT WAS MADE, OR LIKE AN IMAGE THAT WAS REPEATED?**
**Made** — but assembled from a stamp library, not authored.

It genuinely does not tile. I ran shifted mean-absolute-difference across the floor after high-passing out the lighting gradient, horizontal and vertical, at every offset from 3 to 90 px: no periodic minimum, monotonic rise. And the hole decals are not literal copies — best pairwise shape agreement between any two is IoU 0.79, median across all pairs 0.545. Stone lengths vary. So there's no repeated bitmap here and I won't call one.

What it is instead is a base masonry pattern with two decal sets scattered over it by an even random distribution. That's why the room has no places in it. Every square metre carries the same statistical dose of hole and crack. A made floor has a corner nobody goes to and a lane everybody does.

**Q6: WOULD YOU BELIEVE THIS SHIPPED IN A COMMERCIAL GAME?**
YES. The masonry construction is correct, the running bond is right, the torch falloff is handled, it's legible at phone size.

Merely competent. Not genuinely good. It is a well-built floor with nothing to say — it describes a material, not a place, and it does not survive the fiction it's carrying. "Administered, in continuous heavy use, never cared for, held for four hundred years by soldiers who patch everything" should be readable off the ground alone, and none of it is. What I can read off the ground is: sandstone, damaged evenly.

**CULL:** Damage is uniform decorative scatter — the ground records no traffic, no event, and no repair.

**FLIP LIST**
- Multiply the crack and hole-rim layer by the light instead of adding it — at 210+ px from the torch the strokes sit at 2.19× local floor and glow out of the dark; they must fall with it.
- Recolour the cracks dark and let the light do the highlighting: a pale lip only on the torch-facing side, nothing on the far side. Kill the free-floating cream strokes entirely — they read as hairs, not fissures.
- Break the hole size class. Every hole in the room is 14–24 px; add sub-tile pitting and at least one multi-tile collapse with a broken edge and spall radiating from it, so damage has a cause and a scale.
- Author traffic: a lane of worn, seam-softened, tone-lifted flagstone running from the south mouth (x 380–450) through the room, with holes and debris cleared out of it and pushed to the flanks. Right now the ground by the exit is indistinguishable from the ground at the west wall.
- Put the orcs on the floor: two or three driven pins, a salvaged plank bridging a hole, rope lashing across a seam — crude, on top of the stone, breaking the coursing. One or two per room, not scattered.
