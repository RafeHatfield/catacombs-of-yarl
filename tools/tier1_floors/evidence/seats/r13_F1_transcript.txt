**Q1: WHAT IS THIS FLOOR MADE OF?**
Cut sandstone flags, laid in irregular courses with recessed joints.

**Q1_WHY:** Rectangular units of varying width in horizontal courses across x 185–625, y 15–395 — some one unit wide (~32px, e.g. the run at x 190–290, y 145–175), some two or three (~96px, e.g. (445,210)–(525,240)). Each unit is bounded by a darker recessed line ~4px wide on all sides, which is a mortar joint, not a shadow — it holds constant width regardless of position relative to the light. Faces are flat and matte with no bedding, no grain direction, no mineral speckle. I say sandstone rather than granite or slate only because the value is warm and the faces are soft-edged; there is no positive evidence of a specific stone. The hue tells me nothing — I measured the whole floor region and hue sits between 24° and 29°, a five-degree band, saturation 0.38–0.56. That is one colour ramp, which is the lamp's colour, not the rock's.

**Q2: WHICH WAY WOULD YOU WALK, AND WHY?**
South, into the corridor mouth at roughly x 380–435, y 395 and below.

But I have to be straight about the reason: **nothing about the ground influenced that.** I picked it because it is the only visible break in the wall silhouette. The floor gave me no help at all. There is no traffic path, no polished lane, no threshold wear at the corridor mouth, no scuffing, no drag marks, no direction of any kind. I checked the tiles immediately in front of the corridor — (360,360)–(450,395) — against tiles in a dead corner at (200,60)–(290,120), and they carry the same density and the same kind of marking. A floor that has been in continuous heavy use "since before anyone's records begin" should have told me where everybody walks. This one is isotropic.

**Q3: HAS ANYTHING HAPPENED TO THIS FLOOR?**
Almost nothing legible. It reads as *evenly distressed* rather than as damaged, used, or repaired.

Three things are actually present. First, a network of hairline cracks: one arcing from about (340,150) down to (355,250); one running left-to-right from about (230,160) to (335,190); a long one lower-right from roughly (465,300) to (580,315); another across the bottom from about (300,360) to (435,340). Second, per-tile scratch hatching, on nearly every face. Third, several flat dark rectangles.

What is absent is the part that matters. **There is not one repair anywhere on this floor.** No rope, no driven pins, no hide, no salvaged timber, no packing, no patch, no plate, no fill. Four hundred years of orc-soldiers repairing things endlessly with whatever is to hand, and the ground under them is unmended stone. That is the single largest gap between what is drawn and what the place is.

**Q3_WHY:** The cracks are the giveaway on quality. They are one pixel wide, constant width along their whole length, constant value, and they pass through the mortar joints without interacting with them — the crack at (340,150)–(355,250) crosses at least four joints and neither widens, jogs, nor terminates at any of them. They also don't branch. They read as lines drawn on top of the floor, not as the floor having split.

The dark rectangles are worse. I sampled (363,124)–(410,142): mean RGB (123, 87, 54). The mortar joint at (300,113)–(360,118) directly above it: mean (128, 91, 57). They are the same colour. The block at (516,82)–(546,110) measures a standard deviation of 4.7 — dead flat, flatter than the lighting gradient crossing it. These have no rim, no lip, no depth cue, no debris at the edge, and hard grid-aligned corners. They do not read as pits or missing flags. They read as tiles nobody has drawn yet.

**Q4: IS THERE ANYTHING YOUR EYE KEEPS RETURNING TO?**
Yes. Two things.

**The 45° hatch cluster.** It is on nearly every tile face, in one of only two directions, at roughly the same size and density each time. (250,55), (330,40), (350,95), (430,60), (215,190), (285,215), (490,255), (600,80), (520,290) — I stopped listing. Once I saw it I could not stop seeing it, and at 2× the whole floor reads as a *wallpaper of the same scratch mark* rather than as a floor. It pulls attention away from the scene because it is the only thing with any rhythm in the image, and the rhythm is wrong — it is even where it should be clustered.

**The flat mortar-coloured rectangles**, listed above at (363,124)–(410,142), (526,211)–(575,240), (194,292)–(280,325), (516,82)–(546,110). My eye goes to them as holes in the artwork every single pass, because they are the highest-contrast shapes on the floor and they carry zero information.

I'll also note the pale tile at (445,210)–(525,240): it is large, brightly lit, and completely featureless while every neighbour is hatched. Next to the figure, in the best-lit part of the frame, it reads as unfinished.

**Q5: MADE, OR REPEATED?**
**Repeated** — though it is a more careful repeat than usual, so let me be exact about it.

I tested for literal tiling: high-passed the floor to strip the lamp gradient, then correlated the region against itself at offsets of 16, 32, 64, 96 and 128 px in both axes. Best score was 0.22, at a horizontal offset of 64. That is not a blitted texture; the layout is not a stamped grid, and the tile widths genuinely vary. Somebody did lay this out.

But the *surface* is repeated. I ran a near-duplicate search over 2,094 detail patches: 725 of them — 35% — have a near-identical twin (cosine >0.85) somewhere else on the floor at a distance greater than one tile. And the visual read at 2× confirms what the number says: a small vocabulary of marks — the two hatch directions and a horizontal dash stack — sprayed one-per-tile across the whole room. The *arrangement* was made. The *material* was repeated. What is missing is any evidence of individual history: no tile that is different because something happened to it in particular.

**Q6: WOULD YOU BELIEVE THIS SHIPPED IN A COMMERCIAL GAME?**
**YES.** It would pass without comment in a mid-tier roguelike.

Is it genuinely good? **No.** It is competent. The layout logic is real, the joint work is clean, the value range is controlled, and the tile-width variation is better than most. That is craft. But it does not do the one thing the fiction needs — it does not tell you that anyone has ever been here, or that anyone has ever fought to hold it. The cracks are decoration, the wear is uniform, the repairs are absent. Competent, and not good.

**Q7: WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY SEE ON THE SURFACE AT 1:1?**

**Legible at 1:1:**
- The joint grid, clearly. Course lines and tile divisions, everywhere.
- Tile-to-tile value variation — each flag sits at a slightly different lightness. This is the strongest surface signal at 1:1 and it is the only one carrying the floor.
- The long cracks, but only the two nearest the lamp: the (340,150)–(355,250) arc and the (300,360)–(435,340) run. The lower-right one at (465,300)–(580,315) is at the edge of legibility.
- The flat dark rectangles — those read immediately, which is exactly the problem.

**Only appears once you magnify:**
- The 45° hatch clusters. At 1:1, in the *brightest* part of the room, they resolve as a vague scratchiness — I can tell something is there and I cannot tell what. At 4× they are unmistakably chisel-hatching. That is hunting, not seeing.
- The horizontal dash stacks (e.g. (500,90), (215,180), (555,290)) — completely illegible at 1:1, read as smudge.
- Small chips and single dark pixels along tile edges. Invisible at 1:1.

**The faces are effectively blank at 1:1 across most of the room.** In the bright pool within about 120px of the figure, they carry a texture impression but no readable mark. Beyond that — the whole of x 185–300 and x 540–625, and everything below y 320 — the faces go to flat brown and there is nothing on them at all.

**Q7_WHY:** Checked at 1:1: the full frame; then the crop (300,80)–(560,300), which is the best-lit ground in the image and my fairest test. Magnified (4×–6×): (185,20)–(365,160); (330,120)–(510,260); (230,260)–(410,400); (450,180)–(630,320); and the dark blocks at (350,100)–(430,160), (440,200)–(570,260), (215,275)–(330,340), (495,60)–(570,125).

**Q8: HOW OLD IS THIS FLOOR?**
It reads as **one age everywhere: roughly a century, and evenly so.** Not older than the records. Not new.

That uniformity is itself the finding. In a place administered without care and in continuous heavy use, this floor should be several ages at once — worn glassy along the line between the corridor mouth and wherever people go, near-pristine in the dead corner behind the north wall, and patched at whatever failed and got fixed. Instead every square foot of it is aged by the same amount.

**Q8_WHY:** Compared the tiles at (360,360)–(450,395), directly in the mouth of the only exit and therefore the most-trodden ground in the room, against (200,60)–(290,120), a corner nothing has reason to cross. Same hatch density, same edge condition, same joint depth. Then checked (445,210)–(525,240) — a large, well-lit, entirely unmarked face — sitting immediately beside heavily hatched neighbours at (490,255) and (430,190), with no reason for the difference. The century estimate comes from the crack network (roughly (340,150)–(355,250) and (465,300)–(580,315)): present, but hairline and unbranched, which is early-life settlement, not centuries of load. Nothing in the image reads older than that, and nothing reads younger.

**CULL:**
Four hundred years of constant repair, and not one repair is drawn on the floor.
