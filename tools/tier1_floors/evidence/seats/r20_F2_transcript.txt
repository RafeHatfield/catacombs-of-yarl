## Q1: WHAT IS THIS FLOOR MADE OF?
Cut stone slabs — sandstone or limestone ashlar, laid in horizontal courses, dry-ish jointed. Sand/ochre in colour.

**Q1_WHY:** The lit area (x 190–632, y 0–268) is divided by a continuous network of 1–2px near-black joints into rectangular units roughly 22–26px tall and 18–50px wide, arranged in horizontal courses with staggered vertical joints — a running-ashlar bond, not brick, not tile, not flags. The faces are a uniform sand hue: sampling face pixels across the whole lit room, normalised chromaticity sits at r=0.42–0.45 in every cell of a 10×7 grid. One stone, everywhere. The art is authored at 2× (pixel values arrive in pairs — `161 161 163 163` along y=127), so an "art pixel" is 2 screen px.

## Q2: WHICH WAY WOULD YOU WALK, AND WHY?
South, down the passage at x 378–443. **But nothing about the ground influenced that choice.** I picked it off the walls — it is the only opening whose boundaries I can actually see. The ground inside that passage is indistinguishable from the ground in the middle of the room: same course height, same slab widths, same crack scribbles, same colour. If you deleted the walls I would have no reason to go south rather than north or west.

## Q3: HAS ANYTHING HAPPENED TO THIS FLOOR?
It's been comprehensively cracked and pocked, evenly, all over, and then left alone. Nothing has *happened* to it in the sense of an event with a location — it is uniformly damaged the way wallpaper is uniformly patterned.

**Q3_WHY:** I measured dark-linework coverage in 42×44px cells across the lit room. Every cell reads between 16% and 40%, mean ~26%. There is no quiet cell and no dense cell. Roughly a quarter of the visible floor's area is black line, distributed flat. Cracks appear on nearly every slab; I count ~22 dark rosette/pock blobs in the lit area (e.g. (407,106), (427,170), (337,172), (261,226), (489,206), (585,74)) — about one every 60px. And critically: **zero repair.** No patched slab, no replacement stone, no timber, no driven pin, no rope. For a place held and endlessly mended for four hundred years, the floor shows the orcs have never touched it.

## Q4: IS THERE ANYTHING YOUR EYE KEEPS RETURNING TO?
Two things.

1. **The rosettes.** Spiky ~18–22px starburst blobs, ~22 of them across the lit floor — (407,106), (427,170), (405,48), (489,206), (261,226), (299,202), (585,74), (551,150), (345,146), (475,74)… Template-matching one against the rest gives a best non-self correlation of 0.61, so they aren't literal copies, but they are unmistakably one motif repeated. After the third one you stop reading them as damage and start reading them as a sprinkle. They're also the same hue as the floor, just darker (RGB 121,87,57 vs face 208,160,95) — so you can't tell whether they're stains, pits, or holes.

2. **The linework weight.** Joints and cracks are drawn at the *same* colour and *same* width. Cross-section at x=300, y=100–135: joint pixels 35–68 against a face of 108–112 — a ~60% value drop. Cracks measure identically. The eye cannot separate "how this floor was built" from "how it broke." The whole lit area reads as one black net thrown over a sand-coloured field.

## Q5: MADE, OR REPEATED?
**Made** — and this is the floor's one real strength. I autocorrelated the binarised joint mask over the lit room (x190–630, y5–265): horizontal lags 8–70 sit flat at 0.21–0.26, vertical lags decay monotonically to ~0.0 by lag 45. No periodic peak anywhere. Slab widths genuinely vary, courses genuinely stagger, the crack routing is bespoke. Nobody stamped a 16px tile down and walked away. The layout was drawn.

## Q6: WOULD YOU BELIEVE THIS SHIPPED IN A COMMERCIAL GAME?
**YES.**

Merely competent. Not genuinely good. The craft is real — correct 2× scale discipline, disciplined palette, non-repeating layout, a light falloff that reads. But it is one idea (dark lines on flat fill) executed at one density across the entire surface, and it does not survive the second look.

## Q7: WHAT CAN YOU ACTUALLY SEE ON THE SURFACE ITSELF, AT 1:1?
**The faces are blank at 1:1.** What is legible is: joints, cracks, and rosettes — all of which are the *outlines between and on top of* the material, not the material. The stone face itself carries nothing you can see.

There *is* surface detail — chisel dashes and combing — but it is invisible at 1:1. Finding it required a 7–14× magnification with a 14× contrast boost. That is hunting, not seeing.

**Q7_WHY:**

*Checked at 1:1, blank:*
- (370,115)–(465,145) — I dumped the raw row at y=127, x=372–462: `162 163 163 164 … 166 166 166 … 155`. A monotonic lighting ramp, 45px wide, with literally zero material variation. The only breaks are joint pixels (88, 78, 67, 64).
- (300,48)–(330,70), (440,45)–(465,70), (315,225)–(340,245) — sliding 9×9 windows here return high-pass σ of 0.45–0.56 on well-lit stone. Dead flat.
- (250,40)–(340,110), (490,55)–(560,95) — same.

*Only visible under magnification:*
- (430,120)–(462,140): four clusters of 2px-tall horizontal chisel dashes. Measured value 151–155 against a face of 161–166 — a delta of ~10 on a base of 165, **~6% luminance modulation**. The joints are at 60%. The surface detail is ten times weaker than the linework.
- (495,50)–(529,76): the same dash motif again.
- (200,155)–(234,181): dense vertical combing / hatching.
- (255,120)–(289,146): arcs and scratch strokes.

Across all face interiors ≥3px from any dark mark (34% of the lit area), mean deviation is 5.7L on a base of 117 — 4.9%. On a phone, that is nothing.

## Q8: HOW OLD IS THIS FLOOR?
**It reads as one age everywhere: a few centuries old, damaged uniformly at some point, and unwalked ever since.** A ruin, not a working floor.

That is the wrong answer for this fiction. A route in continuous heavy use since before the records would be *two* ages at once — ancient stone, freshly polished. Nothing here is fresh, and nothing here is worn.

**Q8_WHY:** The age signal is carried entirely by crack density, and crack density is constant (16–40% coverage, mean 26%, in every cell across x192–632, y5–265). Age evidence that is *absent*:
- No dishing or polish along any line of travel — face chromaticity is invariant (r=0.42–0.45) across the entire lit floor; there is no darker, cleaner, or brighter band anywhere.
- No rounded joint edges. Every joint at (300,48)–(560,95) and (250,180)–(560,250) is the same crisp 1–2px hard line, corner to corner.
- No differential decay: the slabs at the room's corners (210,180), (560,215) are exactly as damaged as the slabs under the figure (400,170).
- No stratigraphy — nothing has been re-laid, so nothing is younger than anything else.

## Q9: CAN YOU SEE WHERE THE ROUTE GOES?
**The ground told me nothing. I read the route off the walls.**

Looking at the ground alone out to the edge of the light (~220px radius, giving up around x<200, x>620, y>260): there is no line of travel. The coursing runs east–west uniformly, in the room *and* in the south passage — it does not turn to follow the corridor, does not narrow, does not change. There is no polished lane, no scuffed band, no swept centre with debris pushed to the sides, no directional grain of any kind.

**Q9_WHY:**
- *Route I'd take* — (378,250)→(443,600), the south passage. Ground there: identical E–W courses, slabs ~22px tall, one crack running down the middle at x≈400. Face chromaticity r=0.449, matching the room.
- *Rejected* — west along y 60–140 toward x<200, and north through (250,0)–(600,0). Ground there: identical E–W courses, identical slab sizes, identical face chromaticity (r=0.437–0.443 at the western cells, 0.429–0.444 at the northern).
- **What is different about the ground between them: nothing measurable.** That is the finding.

Worse, at the threshold itself — (378,250)–(443,275), the mouth of the south passage — the courses run *straight through* the wall line with no break, no sill, no dip, and the two slab courses immediately inside the mouth are among the *cleanest* on screen: no cracks at all. The one place that must have taken the most traffic is the least marked.

## Q10: WHICH WAY IS USED MOST, WHICH LEAST?
**They all look the same.** Judging only from the ground in front of each: the south passage mouth (x 378–443, y 250–275), the western ground (x 195–260, y 55–140), and the northern ground (x 260–600, y 0–30) are indistinguishable in coursing, slab size, crack density, and colour. There is no most-used and no never-used. Every opening in this dungeon appears to receive exactly the same traffic, which is to say none.

## CULL
Slab faces are blank at 1:1; all detail is a uniformly dense black line-net that reads as noise.

## FLIP LIST
- Split the mark hierarchy: drop joints to a mid-tone (roughly value 110–125 against a 165 face, not 65) and reserve near-black solely for genuine breaks, so construction and damage stop reading as one graphic.
- Raise face detail from ~6% to ~20% contrast so chisel dashes and combing at (430,120), (495,50), (200,155) are legible at 1:1 without magnification — and vary the motif per slab; the dash-cluster currently repeats.
- Cut crack coverage by half in the room interior and concentrate it, so the 26%-everywhere density becomes something like 10% in most cells and 45% in two or three, giving the floor places instead of a pattern.
- Put a worn route through it: a 2–3 slab-wide lane along (400,0)→(410,600) with cracks suppressed, joints eroded shallower, and faces lifted ~8–10% in value — polish from traffic — and let untrodden ground at the room's flanks stay rough.
- Add orc repair to the walkable ground: at least two replacement slabs of visibly different cut and tone, and one timber-and-driven-pin patch, sited in front of the south mouth at (378,250)–(443,290).
