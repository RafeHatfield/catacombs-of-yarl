**Q1: WHAT IS THIS FLOOR MADE OF?**
Dried, cracked packed earth — sun-baked hardpan or mud-flat — heavily overgrown with green moss.

**Q1_WHY:**
The crack network is the giveaway: irregular closed polygons with thin dark seams and no mortar, no tooled edges, no rectilinear slabs anywhere in the room (see the crazing at 3× around 500–620 px x, 200–330 px y). South, down the dark corridor at x≈400–460 below y=400 — **and I chose it at random.** Nothing about the ground influenced it. **Q3: HAS ANYTHING HAPPENED TO THIS FLOOR?**
Weather has happened to it. It has dried out and cracked, and moss has grown over roughly a fifth of it. Nothing else has happened to it. It is undisturbed ground with an even coat of growth — a floor in a place that has been in continuous heavy use since before records began should not look untouched.

**Q3_WHY:**
Three specific readings. (1) The moss is *inverted*: coverage within 24 px of the walls is 12.6%, interior is 18.1%. Real damp growth accumulates at wall bases, in corners, in the cracks, where feet don't reach — here it's thinnest exactly where it should be thickest and thickest in the middle of the room where traffic would kill it. It's scattered noise, not a growth. (2) Column-by-column moss density bounces 9–25% with no gradient in any direction — no water source, no fall line, no dry side. Merely competent. **CULL:**
Warm sunlit mossy earth with visible tile repeats — reads as outdoor ruin, not an administered underworld.
