Read the image, then measured it — the interesting findings here are the ones that survive a pixel test.

## Q1: WHAT IS THIS FLOOR MADE OF?

**Rectangular dressed slabs laid in running bond, with recessed dark joints — but I can only name the *format*, not the *substance*.**

I can tell you it's cut rectangular blocks about one tile tall, offset course to course, set with sunken joints. I cannot tell you whether they're stone, fired brick, or rammed earth blocks. Nothing in the pixels decides it.

**Q1_WHY:** The bond is legible and regular. Horizontal joint lines sit at y = 15, 47, 79, 111, 143, 175, 207, 239, 271, 303, 335, 367 — exactly 32px apart, one grid cell. Joints are a uniform 4px dark recess. Blocks are mostly 32px square with occasional double-width (64px) slabs, staggered roughly half a block per course.

What's *missing* is what would name the material: there is no grain, no bedding plane, no mineral variation, no aggregate, no chip on any corner. The only intra-slab detail is scattered 2×2 dark dots that read as generic pitting. A slab at (452,120) is a flat field of one value plus dots; so is a slab at (556,272). Strip the joints and you could not say what you were standing on.

## Q2: WHICH WAY WOULD YOU WALK, AND WHY?

**South, down the corridor at x≈376–438 — and the ground contributed almost nothing to that choice.**

Two exits: the south corridor, and a west passage at x<180, y≈145–235 (unlit floor, four more holes visible in it at (77,164), (27,229), (69,243), (159,225)).

The only ground-derived reason I have is negative: the west floor sits so close in value to the surrounding blocks that I can't read it as walkable ground, while the corridor floor is legible. That's a lighting-contrast fact, not a property of the surface.

The one genuine ground signal in the whole frame is the hole at (331,159)–(344,176) — the only hole with a straight 2px black tail running south to y≈200, plus two detached 4×4 black squares below it at ~(336,188) and (348,187). It's the single mark that implies a direction. It also happens to be the most artificial-looking thing on the floor (see Q4).

Everything else is directionless. No trodden lane, no lighter path where feet have polished the slabs, no debris drifted against a wall, no accumulation at either doorway. If I remove the lighting, **the ground does not vote.**

## Q3: HAS ANYTHING HAPPENED TO THIS FLOOR?

**It has been punctured, repeatedly, by one thing — and it has never been walked on.**

Those are two separate statements and both are visible.

**Q3_WHY:**

*Punctured.* I count **22 hole-blobs** on one phone screen (excluding the `Msg` button). Their bounding boxes: 22×18, 12×18, 20×22, 18×18, 12×20, 20×18, 20×22, 24×34, 20×18, 20×20, 10×18, 20×20, 20×20, 18×20, 22×18, 18×20, 20×20, 18×20, 18×18, 20×18, 14×18, 20×18. **Every single one is ~20×20 px** — 0.6 of a slab. Not one hairline. Not one whole missing slab. Not one large collapse. Twenty-two events, one calibre. Interiors are flat `#090603` with no depth, no lip, no visible edge thickness. They read as stickers, not openings.

*Never used.* Every joint runs perfectly straight at a uniform 4px. Every slab corner is square. No slab is sunk, tilted, cracked out, replaced with a mismatched one, or wedged back with timber. There is no dirt packed into any joint. Where the room meets the corridor at y≈410 — the one place all traffic funnels — the surface is pixel-for-pixel the same treatment as the middle of the room.

For a place in continuous heavy use since before records begin, and repaired for four hundred years with rope and driven pins, this floor shows **damage without history**. Something shot it. Nobody ever crossed it.

## Q4: IS THERE ANYTHING YOUR EYE KEEPS RETURNING TO?

Three things, and the first two are measurable.

**1 — The pale hook strokes, and specifically the fact that they get *brighter as the room gets darker*.**

Same comma/hook/candy-cane shape everywhere: (373,51)–(396,78); (495,125)–(518,142); (347,147)–(368,206); (449,211)–(500,238); (309,211)–(328,238); (441,23)–(456,46); and — most conspicuously — down in the unlit corridor around (395,545)–(420,590), where a pale hook is one of the most legible things on the whole screen.

I measured decal brightness against the local floor by distance from the lamp:

| distance from lamp | decal | local floor | ratio | **delta** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–60px | 153.1 | 121.3 | 1.27× | **+31.8** |
| 60–110px | 143.3 | 110.5 | 1.30× | **+32.8** |
| 110–160px | 112.2 | 83.4 | 1.36× | **+28.8** |
| 160–210px | 86.2 | 54.8 | 1.61× | **+31.4** |
| 210–300px | 53.0 | 26.2 | **2.06×** | **+26.8** |

The delta is constant. **The decals are added to the lit floor, not multiplied by the light.** Under the lamp they barely read at 1.27×; out in the dark they're twice the brightness of the stone they sit on. That is exactly backwards, and it's why my eye keeps getting dragged into the corners.

The same bug makes the pale halo ring every hole — full strength on all four sides, including the side facing away from the only light in the world.

**2 — The uniform hole calibre.** Once I noticed the 20×20, I couldn't stop counting them. 22 of them, evenly spread, no clustering, no clear ground.

**3 — The tail on the hole at (331,159).** A perfectly straight, 2px-wide, non-tapering black line running down to y≈200 with a hard flat end, plus two floating 4×4 black squares. It crosses the joint at y=175 without changing. It doesn't pool, wander, or thin. It reads as a stray drawn line, not a spill.

## Q5: MADE, OR REPEATED?

**Repeated.** Four independent measurements say so.

- **The floor is one hue.** Across 105,711 floor pixels (player and UI excluded), hue spans **26.2°–29.5°** — a 3° band — and saturation 0.47–0.57. Value ranges 0.24–0.86. Stone, mortar, cracks, chalk marks, hole rims: every one of them is the identical orange-brown at a different brightness. This is a greyscale detail map multiplied by a single tint. Nothing on this floor is a *different colour* from anything else on this floor.
- **22 hole blobs, one size.** Not pixel-identical (best pairwise IoU ~0.82) — so they're procedurally varied — but varied around a single idea at a single scale.
- **Three or four stroke shapes.** Template-matching the pale marks returns non-self IoU up to 0.84 across the room. Same generator, same length, same curvature, same density in a corridor as in an open room.
- **Per-slab flat random albedo.** Sampling slab medians along a course: row y=112 runs 74, 82, **108**, 88, 90, **133**, 122, 116; row y=48 runs 86, 93, 101, 95, 101, **66**, 94. That's a ±30 value swing per slab with no spatial logic — **the same magnitude as the lamp's entire falloff across four tiles.** Blur the image and the floor is a quilt of random rectangles, not a lit room.

A made surface accumulates from causes. This one is a tint over a noise field with three stamps scattered on top.

---

## Q6: WOULD YOU BELIEVE THIS SHIPPED?

**YES.** The bond is correct, the joints are clean, the pixel discipline is consistent, the read at phone size is clear. Plenty of shipped roguelikes look worse.

**Merely competent.** Not good. Good would mean I could name the stone, read where people walk, and tell what made the holes. I can't do any of the three.

---

## CULL

Twenty-two identical black blobs and a single 3° hue: damage stamped on, not a surface anyone used.

---

## FLIP LIST

- Multiply the pale marks and hole halos by the lightmap instead of adding them — measured constant +30 at every distance, so scratches read at 2.06× the floor in the dark corners and 1.27× under the lamp.
- Break the calibre: replace two-thirds of the 22 blobs with a range — hairline joint gaps, one chipped corner, one whole slab missing to a dark void with visible edge thickness — and cluster the survivors instead of spreading them evenly.
- Break the hue lock: give the floor at least two stone tones and cool the joint shadows off the 26–29° band, so mortar, stone and damage aren't the same colour at different brightness.
- Cut per-slab random albedo from ±30 to roughly ±8 so the lamp's falloff, not the noise, owns the value structure; keep large tone shifts for deliberate patches only.
- Put history at the doorway (y≈410) and along the corridor centre: polished slabs on the traffic line, dirt packed into the joints away from it, debris drifted at the wall bases.
