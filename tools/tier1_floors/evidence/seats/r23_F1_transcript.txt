**Q8: HOW OLD IS THIS FLOOR?**
It reads as **two ages that contradict each other**, and the contradiction is the answer.

**Under the lamp — (270–420, 100–270) — it reads as laid last year.** Square corners, faces flat and unmarked, joints uniform, no dishing, no dirt. Then someone drew century-old fractures across it. That's the conflict: the cracks assert great age and the surface they're drawn on refuses to corroborate it. The cracks lose, because they're the thing that reads as ink.

**In the dim band — (470–660, 60–250) and (190–470, 0–55) — it reads older**, maybe a few centuries: the faces are covered in marks. But I only know that because I brightened the image. At the size the game is played, that age is not communicated.

So: the parts you can see read new, and the parts that read old you can't see. Nowhere on this floor does it read as older than the records — nothing here has been in continuous heavy use for an unrecorded span. Heavy use leaves a shape, and this floor has no shape.
