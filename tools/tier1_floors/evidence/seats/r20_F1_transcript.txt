Second, the crack lines are hard-edged 1–2px near-black with nothing either side of them — no lighter lip, no dust, no shadow, no chipped-out mouth where the fracture opens. A crack in stone has a bottom you can't see and edges you can; these have neither.

They share one gesture: a long shallow arc or a long shallow diagonal, low curvature, no branching to speak of, no sharp angular turns. **Q5: MADE, OR REPEATED?**
Made — but barely, and not in the way that helps. The problem is that "not repeated" isn't the same as "made". Nothing repeats because there is almost nothing there to repeat. The floor is a hand-varied lattice drawn on a single flat colour. It has authored *layout* and no authored *surface*.

**Q8: HOW OLD IS THIS FLOOR?**
It reads as one age everywhere — and that age is *laid recently and never used*, with a crack overlay applied afterwards to suggest otherwise.

**Q8_WHY:** The evidence for age is exclusively the crack strands, and I've already said why I don't believe them (Q3_WHY). Set them aside and every other age signal points the other way:

A floor in continuous heavy use since before the records should have at least two ages visible in it — the original and whatever was done to it since. This has one, and the one is "new".

**CULL:**

**FLIP LIST**
- Put legible detail on the slab faces themselves — pitting, tooling marks, grain, tonal breakup at an amplitude visible on a phone. Current face variation is ~1.5% contrast at 4px; it needs to be several times that to survive the screen.
- Make the cracks stop at the joints or run along them. Every strand that crosses a vertical joint uninterrupted (e.g. x≈446, y≈222) has to be broken there, and given chipped mouths and a lighter lip so it reads as depth rather than ink.
- Break the single-hue floor. Slabs need to differ from one another in tone and slightly in hue — replaced pieces, damper corners, a patch of different stone. Right now the entire floor is hue 34.0 ± 0.3.
- Add traffic and its absence: a worn, polished, cleaner strip running through the corridor mouth (x 375–438) and out into the room, with debris and dirt accumulated in the joints and at the room edges where feet don't go.
- Add at least one orc repair to the ground — a plate pinned over a broken slab, timber packed into a gap. A four-hundred-year-held floor with no repairs on it contradicts the fiction more than any texture problem does.

Every question I could answer, I answered off the joint lattice or off the walls. Every question that needed the *face* of the material — age, wear, traffic, which door gets used — came back empty. Fix the faces and most of the flip list dissolves on its own.
