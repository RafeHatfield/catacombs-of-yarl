YARL — BLIND COMPARATIVE SEAT — WALL DEPTH — v0.1

RUN AS: fresh `claude -p`, cwd OUTSIDE the repo. No repo access. No project memory.
INPUT: the two PNG files in this directory, nothing else.
This process is NOT given the art bible. It gets the fiction, the tone, and these questions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Two images, named A.png and B.png. **They are from two different games.** Each is a top-down
dungeon seen straight from above, drawn on a square grid, and each is shown at the size its own
game is actually played — so the pixels are not the same size in both, and that is not a defect
in either.

You are NOT told which game is which, and you must not guess or speculate about it. Nothing in
your answer should depend on which one you think is which.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PLACE

Both are dungeons. The one being developed is the Paths of the Dead: an underworld that is
ADMINISTERED, not haunted — a filing system for souls. In continuous heavy use since before
anyone's records begin, and no one has ever cared for it. Nothing in it is arranged for
anyone's benefit.

The upper floors are a contested frontier where a company of orc-soldiers has held a line for
four hundred years and cannot advance it. They repair things endlessly, with rope, driven pins,
hide and salvaged timber, and they do not care how the repairs look. Their work is heavy,
over-built and competent. Nothing they make exists to be looked at.

The tone is dry, cold and completely straight. Not spooky-fun. Not adventurous. No sense of
humour about itself. Nothing is ruined; things are used up.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SCOPE — READ THIS AND HOLD TO IT

**The floor field in these captures is a small set of tiles cloned across the scene; per-cell
repetition and absent traffic wear on the floor are known artifacts of the variantless test
corpus and are out of scope — variant families and wear states are tier one's job, not this
round's. The question in front of you is wall depth.**

If your instinct is to write about the floor repeating, note it in one clause under Q6 and then
answer the wall question anyway.

Lighting: in at least one of these images the light is cast by the game engine at run time, not
painted into the art. Do not mark an image down for the light being even, or for darkness at
the edges of the frame. **Do** mark it down if a light direction is PAINTED INTO the stone
itself — one edge of every course highlighted and the opposite edge darkened, in a pattern that
would still be there if the light were switched off.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE QUESTIONS

Q1  DEPTH. Which of the two reads as having depth — walls you could stand beside, with a top
    you are looking down on and a face you are looking at? Answer A, B, BOTH, or NEITHER.

Q2  WHAT IT DOES. If one achieves it and the other does not, name concretely and specifically
    what the successful one is doing that the other is not. Point at pixels: values, widths,
    where things sit relative to each other. "It looks better" is not an answer. If NEITHER
    achieves it, say what both are missing, in the same concrete terms.

Q3  SIDE FACE. Does either image show a side face or a cross-section — a wall's flank, or a cut
    through it — as opposed to only a top surface and a front face? Answer A, B, BOTH, or
    NEITHER, and say which pixels you are pointing at. Be careful and literal here.

Q4  HELD. Show me what holds these walls together, in each image. Name the specific elements and
    say where they are. If nothing holds them together, say so plainly.

Q5  STAGED. Is anything in either image arranged for the viewer's benefit — posed, composed,
    placed to look good? Or does it read as a place that is simply in use? Answer per image.

Q6  GENERIC. Does either read as generic fantasy-dungeon stock — the off-the-shelf version of
    this idea — rather than a specific place with its own rules? Answer per image, one line.
    Put any out-of-scope floor observation here, in one clause.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BAR

You are deciding whether this belongs in a shipped commercial game. A critic who finds no defect
should suspect their own rigour before crediting the work.

"Fine", "acceptable", "good enough", "improved", "solid", "promising", "close", "nearly there",
"has potential", "serviceable", "decent", "workable" are all FAILING verdicts. Hedging is
failing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT FORMAT — follow exactly, no preamble

Q1: <A | B | BOTH | NEITHER>
Q1_WHY: <one line>
Q2: <two to four lines, concrete, pointing at pixels>
Q3: <A | B | BOTH | NEITHER>
Q3_WHY: <one line naming the pixels you mean>
Q4_A: <one line>
Q4_B: <one line>
Q5_A: <one line>
Q5_B: <one line>
Q6_A: <one line>
Q6_B: <one line>
CULL_A: <none | key-light | cannot-read | wrong-projection | noise>
CULL_B: <none | key-light | cannot-read | wrong-projection | noise>
RANK: <the better of the two on wall depth: A or B or TIE>

Then:

FLIP LIST
- <a specific, executable change to the WEAKER image's walls that would close the gap>
- <another>

A flip list item must be actionable by someone redrawing the art. "Needs more depth" is not one.
"The wall's top surface and its front face are within a few values of each other; separate them
so the face sits at roughly half the top's brightness" is.
