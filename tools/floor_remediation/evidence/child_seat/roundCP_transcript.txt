SEPARATOR: The top of the list is crisp, centred, machine-regular ornament that looks newly set and never touched, while the bottom two are the only ones whose surfaces carry irregular incident and read as ground rather than as pattern — though none of them shows a mark left by anyone walking on it.

FLIP LIST
- In F1, F4 and F8, delete the closed dark outline around the central panel entirely and let the panel's own value step separate it from the surrounding stone; if the panel then disappears, the panel is too close in value and should be shifted two or three steps, not re-outlined.
- In F4, delete the blank gradient field and draw actual paving across the whole cell so there is no bare ground for the panel to float on; the square must meet neighbouring stone at a joint, not sit in a gap.
- Author four to six distinct floor tiles plus 90-degree rotations and randomise placement, so that no motif lands at the same position in two adjacent cells; every image in this set is one tile repeated verbatim with zero variation.
- Move features off-centre. In all nine, the motif is centred in its 32x32 cell, which is what makes the grid visible; draw paving stones of varying size whose edges deliberately straddle cell boundaries so stone joints and cell joints stop coinciding.
- Add traffic wear that ignores the grid: a lighter, slightly dished, polished channel about ten pixels wide running down the centre of the corridor, and darker accumulated grime in the two-pixel strip where the floor meets each wall, both cutting straight across stone joints rather than stopping at them.
- Add orc repair to roughly one cell in eight: a cracked flag with a wedge driven into the crack, a missing flag backfilled with rammed rubble, a plank of salvaged timber pinned flat across a hole — heavy, over-built, and not aligned to the stone pattern.
- In F7, remove the darker rim from the worn patches and let each patch be a flat value change against the base with a ragged, non-repeating edge; a worn patch is a change of value, not an outlined shape.
- In F2, F3, F6 and F9, remove the repeating ornamental motif from the floor entirely and replace it with irregular paving; nothing the orcs lay exists to be looked at, so a centred decorative frieze in every cell contradicts the fiction before any craft question arises.
