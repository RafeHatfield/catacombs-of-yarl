FLIP LIST
- Delete the border ringing the panel in F1, F4 and F5 entirely. Do not soften it, remove it. Let the slab's own value sit against the surrounding stone, and carry the joints out to the cell edge so each one meets its neighbour's joint and forms a single network instead of five separate rings.
- Kill the one-motif-per-cell lattice: author six floor variants and offset the bond by half a stone between them, so a joint that starts at x=8 in one cell lands mid-stone in the next. As drawn, F2 and F3 put the identical stone at the identical coordinate in all thirty visible cells.
- Wear the traffic line: on the corridor stones, sand a 12-unit band down the centre — lift it two values, erase the joint detail inside it so joints fade out where feet cross them, and leave joints at full depth only within 4 units of the wall. Right now the middle of the corridor and the edge of the corridor are byte-identical.
- Remove the U notch from F5's slabs, or promote it to a distinct rare tile used on roughly one slab in twelve. An identical centred mark on every slab in the level is what turns a floor into printed paper. Delete the moss with it; nothing wet or green belongs in a dry administrative underworld, and it is repeating on a grid too.
- Pull F3's floor off salmon toward the walls' grey-brown and drop it two values, so that with the engine light switched off the floor is the darkest surface in the frame rather than the brightest.
- Halve the joint contrast on floor tiles relative to the wall's mortar. F2 and F3 draw the ground in exactly the same masonry vocabulary and value range as the wall, so the only thing telling the player what is walkable is the engine's light pool.
- Put the orcs' repairs on the floor, not just the walls: on roughly one cell in twelve, a cracked slab pinned flat with four driven iron pins, or a salvaged timber baulk dropped across a hole and worn smooth on its top edge. There is currently no evidence anywhere on the ground that anyone has ever been down here.
