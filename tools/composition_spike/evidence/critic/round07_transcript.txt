FLIP LIST
- Delete the near-black keyline on the corridor floor plates (currently a closed 1px ring at value 15 against a floor at 135, ten times darker than the surrounding stone and four times harder than any wall mortar joint). Replace it with a rebate: one pixel of floor-tone at ~70% on the plate's inner edge, one pixel at ~115% on the outer edge, and break the ring at two corners per plate so it never closes.
- Settle the two conflicting plate conventions. Within a single corridor one plate is ringed in near-black and the next in mid-brown double line, for what is meant to be the same object. Keep the mid-brown rebate, delete the black-ringed variant.
- Break the floor cycle. The vertical corridor currently runs plate / faint-rectangle / square-outline repeated five times end to end, and the horizontal corridor alternates two tiles for twelve tiles unbroken. Author eight floor variants, place them so no motif repeats within three tiles, and leave 30% of tiles carrying no motif at all.
- Wear a path into the corridor floor: a two-to-three-pixel band down the tile centres, one step lighter and with the plate edges rounded off where it crosses them, so the traffic that has run through here for four hundred years is visible in the stone.
- The maroon horizontal straps on the wall faces are 1–2px lines lying flat on the brick with no fixing at either end. Give each one a driven pin at both ends — a 2px light dot with a 1px dark pixel beneath it — and let the strap overlap the brick it crosses so a sliver of the brick is hidden behind it.
- Give the wall a top. The perimeter darkening is currently identical on the edge facing the player's lamp and the edge facing away, which is why it reads as a ring rather than a thickness. Replace it with a 3px cap band drawn in a smoother, paler stone than the wall face, running along the wall's top surface so the cap is a different material from the coursing below it.
- Pick a meaning for the cream L-shapes and rectangles on the brick faces. Either make them chips — irregular, following the brick's own edge, exposing a paler core tone — or make them hardware — straight, with a visible pin and a one-pixel cast shadow. The same shape must not do both jobs.
- Cut one 4x3-tile patch of wall to a different bond: irregular rubble or salvaged timber laid flat as a course, with the brick coursing running into it and stopping dead. Right now all four wall masses are one texture at one scale, so the wall has no history of being broken and refilled.
- Raise the mortar joints' bite to C4's level across the set, and hold the ambient at C1/C2/C4's level rather than C3's — C3's lift flattens the joints and dissolves the edge of the light pool.
