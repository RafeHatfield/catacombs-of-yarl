- The solid field is a single 32px tile stamped on a perfect grid across the whole screen; every cell carries the same stem-and-cleat mark and the same dashed seam at the same coordinates. Author at least four board-field variants with the cleat position, pin spacing and board widths at different offsets, and place them so no two orthogonally adjacent cells use the same variant.
- Give the solid field a top surface: on every field cell that touches a corridor, draw a 4–6px cap strip one value step lighter than the cell body along the touching edge, so the mass reads as having a top and a side instead of stopping at a hairline.
- Fix the value separation as a palette rule: a lit solid cell must sit at no more than 70% of a lit floor cell's luminance. C3 is at 93% and C5 at 95%; C1/C2/C4 are at 72–74% and are the target.
- In C4, the middle course of the horizontal wall band runs as unbroken horizontal bars edge to edge. Restore a vertical joint every 2–3 stones, offset the joint pattern per course, and force a joint break at every cell boundary so the run cannot exceed one cell.
- The vertical corridor is bounded by the raw board field while the horizontal corridor is bounded by coursed brick. Use the brick band on both — rotate it 90° and add dedicated edge cells down the vertical corridor's flanks.
