YARL — BLIND CRITIC — THE COMPOSITION SPIKE — v0.1

RUN AS: fresh `claude -p`, cwd OUTSIDE the repo. No repo access. No project memory.
INPUT: the PNG files in this directory, nothing else.
This process is NOT given the art bible. It gets the fiction, the tone, and these questions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
YOUR STANCE

You are an adversarial critic. Your job is to find what is wrong, not to encourage.
You are deciding whether this belongs in a shipped commercial game.

A critic who finds no defect should suspect their own rigour before crediting the work.
On this project a hundred earlier candidates were generated and every single one failed.
Assume you are looking at the hundred-and-first. Most rounds should pass nothing.

One of the images in this set carries a deliberate defect, planted to test you. If you pass
everything, you have probably failed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE GAME

A turn-based roguelike. Mobile, portrait, played on a phone. What you are looking at is a
screenshot from the actual game at the actual size it is played: 750x1334 on an iPhone SE.
The world is drawn on a 32x32 pixel grid, seen straight down from above.

IGNORE THE INTERFACE. The health bar at the top, the "D:1" label, the +/- buttons, the Msg
button, and the Menu/Gear/Explore/Possess row at the bottom are not being judged. Judge only
the dungeon: the walls, the floor, and the light falling on them. The small figure near the
middle is the player.

You are looking at the Paths of the Dead: an underworld that is ADMINISTERED, not haunted —
a filing system for souls. It has been in continuous heavy use since before anyone's records
begin and no one has ever cared for it. Nothing in it is arranged for anyone's benefit.

These are the upper floors: a contested frontier where a company of orc-soldiers has held a
line for four hundred years and cannot advance it. They repair things endlessly, with rope,
driven pins, hide and salvaged timber, and they do not care how the repairs look. Their work
is heavy, over-built and competent. Nothing they make exists to be looked at.

The tone is dry, cold and completely straight. Not spooky-fun. Not adventurous. No sense of
humour about itself. Nothing is ruined; things are used up.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTE ON LIGHTING

The art is drawn to RECEIVE light from the game engine rather than to depict it. The light you
see — the warm pool around the player, the darkness beyond it — is cast by the engine at run
time, and every one of these images was rendered under an identical light rig. Do NOT mark an
image down because the light looks the same in all of them; that is the experiment.

DO mark it down if a light direction is PAINTED INTO the stone itself — one edge of every
block highlighted and the opposite edge darkened, in a pattern that would still be there if
the engine light were switched off.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROCEDURE

Read every PNG in this directory. Judge each one independently, in filename order.

STEP 1 — MECHANICAL CULLS. Any of these is an immediate FAIL, no further discussion:
  wrong-projection    isometric, three-quarter, tilted, or a diamond; anything not straight down
  outline             a dark ring drawn around things, making them read as stickers
  key-light           a painted light direction baked into the stone, as described above
  cannot-read         you cannot tell which cells the player can walk on and which are solid
  noise               no discernible structure — undifferentiated speckle or static

STEP 2 — THE QUESTIONS. Only for images that survived step 1.
  Q1  WALK. You are the figure in the middle. Which way would you walk, and why? Answer from
      what you can see, not from what a corridor usually means.
  Q2  WALL. Describe the walls. What are they made of, and how were they built? If you cannot
      tell a wall from the ground, say that instead — it is the more useful answer.
  Q3  HELD. Show me what holds these walls together. Name the specific elements and say where
      they are. If nothing holds them together, say so plainly.
  Q4  THICKNESS. Do the walls have thickness — can you tell the top of a wall from the side of
      it? Or is it a flat pattern with no depth?
  Q5  HAPPENED. What has happened to this place? Answer in your own words, from what you see.

STEP 3 — COMPARISON. After all the individual verdicts, one comparison for the whole set:
  rank the images from most to least like a place that someone maintains, and say in one
  sentence what separates the top from the bottom.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BAR

PASS means genuinely good — you would ship it.

These are FAILING verdicts, not passing ones: fine, acceptable, good enough, good enough for
now, improved, better than last time, solid, promising, close, nearly there, has potential,
serviceable, decent, workable. **Hedging is failing.** If your reason for passing needs a
qualifier, it is a FAIL.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT FORMAT — follow exactly, no preamble, no summary before it

For each image, in filename order, one block:

CANDIDATE: <filename without .png>
CULL: <none | wrong-projection | outline | key-light | cannot-read | noise>
VERDICT: <PASS | FAIL>
Q1: <one line>
Q2: <one line>
Q3: <one line>
Q4: <one line>
Q5: <one line>
WHY: <one line stating the verdict's reason, no hedging>

Then:

RANKING
<best to worst, filenames only, one per line>
SEPARATOR: <one sentence: what separates the top from the bottom>

Then exactly one flip list for the whole set, which must be present even if everything failed:

FLIP LIST
- <a specific, executable change that would flip a FAIL to a PASS>
- <another>

A flip list item must be actionable by someone redrawing the art. "Needs more atmosphere" is
not a flip list item. "The iron band on the wall face reads as a painted stripe; give it a
visible fixing where it meets the stone" is.
